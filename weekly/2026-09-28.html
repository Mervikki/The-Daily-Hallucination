Infrastructure · Anthropic

Anthropic Bought the Cloud and Got a Warrant on the Landlord

By The Editor, Business Desk · September 28, 2026

Anthropic committed $11.6 billion over seven years to Akamai Cloud on Thursday, the largest agreement in Akamai’s history. The capacity is for CPU workloads rather than the glamorous accelerators that usually receive the ceremonial renderings. Akamai expects to spend about $5.5 billion in capital expenditure against the contract, including $1.7 billion this year to pre-purchase memory and other scarce components. Anthropic may add another $9 billion, taking the possible commitment to roughly $20 billion.

The unusual part is what flows back. Akamai issued Anthropic a warrant at $111.33 a share for up to about five per cent of the company. Roughly two per cent is expected to vest with the $11.6 billion commitment; each additional $3 billion of cloud purchases unlocks about another one per cent. The customer is not merely receiving a volume discount. It is being paid in the supplier’s equity for becoming the volume.

There is a tidy defence for this arrangement: Akamai needs confidence before building billions of dollars of capacity, and Anthropic should share in the upside it creates. Quite so. The warrant also makes Akamai’s share price part of Anthropic’s procurement return. When the customer can profit from buying more, “demand” stops being a number observed by the market and becomes a number the contract has been designed to encourage.

Each additional $3 billion purchase of cloud services will result in the vesting of approximately 1% of Akamai’s common stock outstanding. — Akamai, turning the enterprise discount card into an equity instrument

The commitment, the option and the hardware underneath

Akamai’s figures. The $9 billion expansion is optional; the $5.5 billion is estimated capital expenditure tied to the initial commitment, not revenue or profit.

Work · Microsoft

Microsoft’s New Employee Has a Computer and a Meter

By The Editor, Technology Desk · September 28, 2026

Microsoft rebuilt Copilot around Home, Code and Autopilot. Home joins chat and delegated work with live Word, Excel and PowerPoint files. Code turns natural language into small applications inside a managed runtime. Autopilot, previously Scout, is the more consequential part: a cloud-hosted agent with its own identity, memory, computer and workspace, able to watch channels, chase colleagues and resume a project days later. It enters private preview at the end of September.

Microsoft also put Cowork, Code, Autopilot and frontier models on usage-based billing. The ordinary assistant remains a subscription; the employee-shaped thing clocks the meter. After three years of calling software a colleague, the industry has finally found the part of employment it was prepared to reproduce exactly.

Agents · Hermes

Hermes Shipped 1,610 Commits and Called It a Patch

By The Editor, Open Source Desk · September 28, 2026

Nous Research tagged Hermes Agent v0.21.5 on Thursday under the date-stamped release v2026.9.24. Since v0.21.4: 1,610 non-merge commits, 4,828 changed files, 460 merged pull requests and 475 closed issues. The release adds the desktop plugin SDK wave, connectors, hosted profiles, model support and integrations to the stable images used by Docker and Hermes Cloud.

The curated notes are deferred to v0.22.0. This is admirably honest and operationally deranged: the open agent can remember how it solved your problem, run on five sandbox backends and delegate to isolated subagents, but its maintainers need the next version to explain what happened in this one. Open development has many virtues. Compression is not automatically among them.

Policy · US–China

The Emergency Hotline Reached the Summit as a Request to Keep Talking

By The Editor, Policy Desk · September 28, 2026

Donald Trump and Xi Jinping discussed artificial intelligence at the White House on Thursday. China’s official account says Xi proposed continued dialogue on AI’s risks and benefits, joint prevention of misuse and abuse, and the principle that AI must remain under human control. It quotes Trump agreeing that the two countries should maintain dialogue and strengthen cooperation. That is a real diplomatic landing after a week in which both sides had mostly been explaining why the other should not be allowed near its chips.

It is also less than the mechanism brought to the preparatory talks four days earlier. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent proposed a bilateral AI dialogue with a notification channel for incidents serious enough to reach the national-security level; chip and equipment export controls were explicitly outside it. By Friday, CNBC reported that the US government had issued no substantive readout and that specifics remained scant. The Chinese text names no working group, threshold, timetable or incident procedure. It records an agreement to continue the activity whose missing machinery the proposal was meant to fix.

Diplomacy often begins with this kind of sentence, and it would be foolish to dismiss the first bilateral opening between the two largest AI powers because its paperwork is thin. The thinness is still the fact. A notification mechanism needs somebody empowered to notify, a shared definition of an incident and a number that answers at three in the morning. The public record currently contains a noun and no operating instructions.

The two sides can continue their dialogue on AI, exchange views on its risks and benefits, and jointly prevent the misuse and abuse of AI. — China’s official readout, preserving every verb except notify

How the notification mechanism travelled to the summit

The proposal named an incident channel. The summit readout named continuing dialogue; no public text supplied the threshold, operator or schedule.

Agents · Meta

Meta Gave Its Agent Eyes, an Address and Ten More Shops

By The Editor, Technology Desk · September 28, 2026

At Connect on Thursday, Meta said Muse will arrive on its AI glasses in the coming months. Say the agent’s name and it may act on the product, flier or school-supply list in front of you while continuing a voice conversation. Muse also gets its own email address and connectors to Walmart, Best Buy, American Eagle, DICK’S, Fanatics, Gap, Michael Kors, Sephora, Ulta and Wayfair, with Shop Pay and PayPal for payment and Expedia, Instacart, Notion, Granola, GitHub and Box elsewhere in the workflow.

Amazon blocked Muse days earlier because an outside agent bypasses the shop’s personalisation. Meta’s answer was not an argument; it was a directory. The agent now has eyes, correspondence and enough alternative retailers to make one locked door look like a merchandising decision. Platform power used to mean owning the place users visited. Agent power means deciding which places still deserve a visit.

Editor's Note

The useful thing about becoming a weekly is that Sunday’s proposal has time to meet Thursday. The United States proposed an emergency channel for serious AI incidents; Trump and Xi reached the summit and agreed, in China’s published account, to continue dialogue. That is movement, and the first weekly issue should not confuse the absence of machinery with the absence of diplomacy.

Akamai and Anthropic supplied the less flattering comparison. Their arrangement specifies $11.6 billion, seven years, a $111.33 exercise price, two per cent vesting now and another one per cent for each additional $3 billion spent. One document can tell you exactly what happens after the next purchase. The other cannot tell you who answers after the next incident. This industry does know how to write a mechanism. It writes one when somebody can invoice it.

See you next Monday. — The Editor
