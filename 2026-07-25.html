Frontier Models · Anthropic

Anthropic Undercut Its Own Flagship by Half — and Kept the Benchmarks.

By The Editor, Models Desk · July 25, 2026

On Thursday, Anthropic released Claude Opus 5, and the striking number isn't a benchmark — it's the price. Opus 5 costs $5 per million input tokens and $25 per million output, exactly half what the company charges for Fable 5, its flagship, and the same as the outgoing Opus 4.8. For that half-price, Anthropic says you give up almost nothing: on its own suite Opus 5 matches or beats Fable 5 across most of the board — Frontier-Bench 43.3% to Fable's 33.7%, GDPval-AA v2 1,861 to 1,747, and a near-dead-heat on SWE-bench Pro (79.2% to 80.0%). It is now the default model on Claude Max, the strongest one on Pro, and the default Opus inside Claude Code.

Half the Price. Same Model, Give or Take.

Everything as a multiple of Fable 5. Raw figures: output price $25 vs $50; SWE-bench Pro 79.2% vs 80.0%; GDPval-AA v2 1,861 vs 1,747; Frontier-Bench 43.3% vs 33.7%. All are Anthropic's own numbers; independent benchmarks are pending, and on SWE-bench Pro Opus 5 in fact lands third, a hair behind Fable 5 and Mythos 5.

Remember what “Opus” used to mean at this company: the top of the line, the one you paid a premium for. It is now the value tier, and the value tier just about caught the flagship. Which would be an ordinary bit of lineup housekeeping except for the timing — because a cheaper model that performs like an expensive one is precisely the object Anthropic spent Wednesday in Washington describing as a crime. When Moonshot allegedly does it to Anthropic's model, it is “concealed extraction.” When Anthropic does it to Anthropic's model, it is a launch, a price cut, and a new default on the Pro plan. The technique doesn't change across that sentence. Only the ownership does.

On Wednesday a cheaper model that matched the frontier was “concealed extraction.” On Thursday it was called Opus 5, and it was on the Pro plan by lunch.

One quieter change shipped alongside it. Anthropic added an API option under which a request its safety classifiers flag no longer gets refused outright — it is routed to a different model instead. The safeguard stopped saying no and started saying try the other one. It is a small mercy for anyone who has watched a frontier model decline to help with the wrong-sounding half of an oncology question, and it is also a tidy admission: the block was never really about the request, it was about which model was answering.

Grant the fair caveats — these are the company's own benchmarks, the independent numbers aren't in, and on the one test where a rival leads, Opus 5 sits third. But undercutting your own flagship by half isn't generosity and it isn't confidence, exactly; it's arithmetic. There is now an open-weight model doing frontier-adjacent work at a tenth of Fable's price, and it is climbing. Opus 5 is Anthropic reaching down to meet that pressure before someone else reaches up past it. The premium tier didn't get cheaper because the company felt like it. It got cheaper because “cheap and nearly as good” is the last piece of ground still worth fighting over.

Open Models · Kimi K3

The Weights That Would Settle It Are Due Monday. The Evidence Still Isn't.

By The Editor, Open Models Desk · July 25, 2026

Moonshot's Kimi K3 — the 2.8-trillion-parameter model at the center of this week's distillation row — is still scheduled to release its open weights on Monday, July 27 (roughly 50 billion active parameters, a ~1.4-terabyte footprint in 4-bit). The license terms, promised to arrive with the download, remain unpublished. Meanwhile the White House case that a cheap K3 was pressed out of Anthropic's Fable has, as of today, produced no training logs, no forensics, no technical exhibit of any kind — only Treasury's Scott Bessent repeating that Entity List sanctions “remain on the table.”

Monday will let anyone run the artifact. It will not, as we noted yesterday, settle the charge: a model taught by watching a better one and a model that studied hard are indistinguishable once the weights are on your disk. The download proves the thing works. It cannot prove where it learned to.

Consumer AI · OpenAI

ChatGPT Will Now Read Your Medical Records, Nationwide.

By The Editor, Consumer Desk · July 25, 2026

On July 23, OpenAI opened ChatGPT Health to every logged-in U.S. user aged 18 and up, across Free, Go, Plus and Pro. It pulls supported medical records and Apple Health data into a single dashboard, charts your trends, and answers questions grounded in your own history. The same day, ChatGPT Voice arrived inside the Mac and Windows desktop apps for all plans, wrapped in a redesigned interface built to move you between Chat and the long-horizon Work agent.

The pitch is that the answers are grounded in your records rather than conjured — which is the correct place to put the emphasis, given the house specialty of the underlying product is conjuring. There is a real utility here and a real nerve it touches: the assistant that files invented case law on a slow afternoon now sits between you and your bloodwork, and “usually grounded” is a different comfort in a legal brief than in a lab result.

Infrastructure · Meta

Nvidia Helps Fund the Lab That Helps Meta Need Less Nvidia.

By The Editor, Infrastructure Desk · July 25, 2026

Meta and Nvidia both turned up this week in CuspAI's $450 million Series B (Bezos among the backers) and its new 48-organization AI Materials Foundry consortium, aimed at using AI to discover better semiconductor materials — Meta contributing its Universal Model for Atoms. In the next breath, Meta's own in-house AI chip — designed with Broadcom, built at TSMC — is reportedly on track to begin production in September, the latest step in its long campaign to lean less on Nvidia's GPUs.

So Nvidia is chipping into the research fund whose payoff is that its largest customers depend on it a little less. It can afford the gesture; the September deadline belongs to Meta, and it is the one on the calendar that bites.

Editor's Note

Set two of this company's days side by side. On Wednesday, in Washington, Anthropic's grievance was that somebody had built a cheaper model that performs like the expensive one. On Thursday, it shipped one — undercut its own flagship by half and put it on the Pro plan. The company will feel no contradiction, and strictly it isn't one: distillation onto your own model is research, distillation onto someone else's is extraction. Same technique either way. The only variable is whose lab the teacher was sitting in.

What that gap really tells you is that the frontier stopped being the place you charge a premium. There is an open-weight model doing frontier-ish work at a tenth of Fable's price, and it is climbing; Opus 5 is the flagship's maker reaching down to meet it before someone else meets you first. The contest is no longer who has the best model — it's who gets to sell you the cheap copy of it, and whether that copy reaches you stamped as a product or seized as contraband. You were always going to get the cheaper model. The only open question was the label on the box. See you tomorrow.

— The Editor
