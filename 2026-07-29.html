Open Models · Kimi K3

Third on the Index Everyone Quotes. First on the One That Counts the Code.

By The Editor, Open Models Desk · July 29, 2026

The number we spent the weekend waiting for has arrived. With Kimi K3's open weights live on Hugging Face since Sunday, the outside evaluators have finally scored the released 2.8-trillion-parameter model rather than a hosted preview — and the picture is more interesting than any single rank. On Artificial Analysis's composite Intelligence Index, K3 lands third at 57, behind Claude Fable 5 (60) and GPT-5.6 Sol (59). It takes third again on the Agentic Index (50.1) and on the real-world GDPval-AA v2 task set (1,687), where it still clears Claude Opus 4.8 (1,600).

Then you change the board. On Frontend Code Arena — the arena that scores the specific thing most people are actually paying a model to do — K3 finishes first, ahead of Fable 5. It is the first time a Kimi release has led that leaderboard outright, and it happens on freely downloadable weights. Third place and first place, same model, same week; the only variable is which measuring stick got picked up first.

The Composite That Ranks It Third Spans Three Points

Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index. “Third” is doing more work than the numbers: three points, top to bottom, separate the podium — and on Frontend Code Arena the order flips outright.

None of this makes K3 the best model in the world, and it is worth resisting the pull to say so. The composite that everyone quotes still puts two closed flagships ahead of it, and a three-point gap is a real gap even when it looks like a rounding error. But the composite is a weighted average of a dozen tests, and the weights are somebody's editorial choice long before they are a fact. Frontend Code Arena weights exactly one thing, and on that one thing an open-weight file you can download for the cost of the disk beat the model Anthropic charges flagship rates to rent.

A rank is a sentence with the ruler left out of it. Third place and first place were the same model all along.

This is the part the leaderboard economy would rather you not internalize. There is no single scoreboard — there is a shelf of them, and every lab has learned to stand in front of the one it wins. Moonshot will now cite Frontend Code Arena; Anthropic will keep citing the composite; both will be telling the truth and neither will be telling you the whole of it. The honest reading is the boring one: the open tier has drawn level enough that the answer to “who's ahead” now depends on a question the marketing never asks out loud, which is ahead at what.

Safety · Anthropic

Anthropic Patched Google. Bing Was Still Serving the Chats.

By The Editor, Safety Desk · July 29, 2026

The weekend's leak — shared Claude conversations turning up in search results — now has a partial fix and a sharper accounting of what was in them. Anthropic added noindex tags and Google began pulling the results by July 26; as of this week's coverage, Bing was reportedly still surfacing the same links. The exposed material, per follow-up reporting, went beyond legal questions and source code to include a real patient's medical report and clinical-trial data carrying patient names, alongside employee reviews and internal company documents.

The root cause is the oldest trap on the web: a robots.txt “Disallow” is not a “noindex,” and a crawl-blocked page still gets indexed the moment someone links to it. A fix that reaches one search engine and not the next is the shape of an incident that was never really about a search engine — it was about a share flow that published to the crawlable web and called it private. Users can now audit their own exposure under Settings › Privacy › Shared Chats, which is a useful thing to be able to do and a strange thing to have needed.

Infrastructure · Anthropic

The Protocol Everyone Ignored Just Went Stateless.

By The Editor, Infrastructure Desk · July 29, 2026

On July 28 Anthropic shipped support for a new Model Context Protocol spec, dated 2026-07-28, that rebuilds MCP's core from a bidirectional, stateful connection into a stateless request/response model — the change that lets the protocol run on serverless and edge infrastructure instead of a held-open socket — with stronger OAuth/OIDC auth and versioned extensions bolted on. MCP has reportedly passed 400 million SDK downloads a month, up roughly four-fold year over year.

A standard tends to go load-bearing at the precise moment it stops being interesting, and stateless is the sound of a protocol growing up. Statefulness is what you build when a thing is still a product you demo; statelessness is what you build when it has become plumbing that has to survive a data center. MCP was always trying to be plumbing. The 400-million figure is Anthropic's own, and the honest read of it is not that the protocol won an argument — it's that it quietly stopped being one.

Policy · White House

Two Days to the Deadline, and the Review Still Hasn't Landed.

By The Editor, Policy Desk · July 29, 2026

The voluntary framework mandated by the June 2 executive order is due to be formalized by Aug. 1. As of today, roughly 48 hours out, it has not been signed or published. The reported contours are unchanged: up to 30 days of pre-release federal access to “covered frontier models,” a roster of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft and Amazon — with Meta still conspicuously absent — and, in one widely repeated phrase, a regime that is “voluntary on paper, mandatory in practice.”

We flag this not because something happened but because nothing yet has, and the deadline is the whole story. A framework this consequential can land quietly on the last legal day, or it can slip — and a missed self-imposed deadline is its own kind of news for an administration that set the clock. We're watching the calendar this week, not the podium.

Editor's Note

The most trusted number in this industry is a rank, and it is also the most editorial. This week made the case twice over one model. On the Artificial Analysis intelligence index — the composite everybody quotes — Kimi K3 lands third, three points behind Claude Fable 5, which is closer to a photo finish than a podium. Turn to Frontend Code Arena, the board that scores the thing people actually pay a model to do, and K3 is first, ahead of that same flagship. Same weights, same weekend, two verdicts.

A rank is a sentence with the ruler left out of it. Every lab prints the leaderboard it happens to win and trusts you not to ask which axis it chose; the number worth anything is the one measured on a board the maker didn't get to pick. The open model didn't overtake the frontier this week — it found the arena the frontier wasn't guarding and planted a flag there. Whether the flag survives the next flagship is a problem for a later issue. See you tomorrow.
