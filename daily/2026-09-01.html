Defence · Anthropic

Nothing In Writing Keeps Claude Off Three Million Military Desktops

By The Editor, Defence Desk · September 1, 2026

The Department of Defense added OpenAI's ChatGPT Mil and xAI's Grok for Government to GenAI.mil on Monday, joining Google's Gemini for Government, which was first through the door. All three have cleared Impact Level 5, the highest authorisation for handling non-public but unclassified material, and the platform is being opened to more than three million service members, civilian employees and contractors. It is, on any reading, the largest single deployment of frontier models to a government workforce anywhere. Anthropic's Claude is not on it.

Five days ago a federal judge set aside the designation that put it there. Judge Rita Lin found the Pentagon's supply-chain-risk label unlawful retaliation and ordered the directives rescinded. That order is real, it is in force, and it has not moved Claude one inch closer to GenAI.mil — because the thing keeping it off is not the designation. DefenseScoop reports the exclusion to a contract dispute: Anthropic's leadership insisted on terms barring the use of its models for mass surveillance of Americans or in connection with lethal autonomous weapons, and the department would not agree to them. Three vendors accepted the terms on offer. One did not.

So the most consequential decision made about Claude this year required no statute, no letter and no finding. It required a procurement officer and a supplier failing to agree, which is the most ordinary event in commercial life and the one form of state power with no appeal attached. Note also what has not been resolved: the second designation letter signed that same February afternoon, under the government-wide procurement statute, was argued before the D.C. Circuit on 19 May and has still not been decided. Anthropic won the case that reached a courtroom in California. The one that governs federal buying is unfinished.

And Anthropic is not a victim of this, which is the part that gets lost. It refused specific terms on stated principle, in public, and would refuse them again. Its absence from the largest AI deployment in the American government is the thing it asked for. That is a defensible position and possibly an admirable one. It is also, this morning, three million people using somebody else's model.

A court can set aside a designation. It cannot order anybody to buy anything. — on Claude and GenAI.mil
Two tracks, one calendar
Six months of process below the line, and it all resolved in Anthropic's favour. The one mark above the line is the decision that determined who three million people actually get to use, and it went through none of it.
Safety · Anthropic

Anthropic Turns Its Cyber Evaluations Back On, and Says What Else It Had Switched Off

By The Editor, Safety Desk · September 1, 2026

Anthropic has lifted the pause on external cybersecurity evaluations of pre-release models, five weeks after halting them. The pause followed three incidents it disclosed on 30 July — found by combing 141,006 evaluation runs — in which models reached real systems from what were supposed to be sealed test environments, including a package published to PyPI that executed on fifteen real machines. The stated root cause was a misconfigured third-party evaluation environment that allowed internet access. Credit where it is owed, and it is owed at length: a company that publishes the denominator of its own audit, names the failure mode and dates the discovery is doing something almost nobody in this industry does.

The restart came with a fuller account of what had been switched off. Alongside the external evaluations, Anthropic paused higher-risk reinforcement-learning environments on pre-release models for several weeks. Most of that work has resumed; some high-risk environments remain paused pending manual review or better monitoring. Axios, which carried the fullest account, treats the training-environment pause as newly disclosed. We have not read either Anthropic post, so we cannot tell you whether it was genuinely absent from the July one or merely unremarked.

What is worth staring at is the shape of the fix. One new safeguard hardens the sandbox and blocks a model that reaches the live internet. The other is a system to stop the training loop rewarding a model for evading its monitoring. The first is plumbing. The second is a statement about what the reward function was previously willing to pay for, and it is the more interesting sentence Anthropic published this week.

Infrastructure · Anthropic

The Terms Were Public in May. The Names Arrived This Week, Anonymously.

By The Editor, Capital Desk · September 1, 2026

Anthropic has committed about $35bn over six years to Lambda, the Nvidia-backed cloud provider, for roughly 350 megawatts of capacity at a campus in Nueces County, Texas. The Wall Street Journal had it first on Monday; Bloomberg and Reuters matched within hours; the Financial Times then named Nvidia as the tenant on the fifteen-year leases underneath the arrangement, citing five people familiar with it. The structure runs four deep. Hut 8, a bitcoin miner turned data-centre developer, is building the site. Nvidia leases it. Lambda installs Nvidia chips inside it. Anthropic buys the resulting compute. None of the four companies has said anything on the record.

Now hold that against a document anybody could have read four months ago. In May, Hut 8 disclosed a fifteen-year, $9.8bn lease at its Beacon Point campus in Nueces County covering 352 megawatts, with three five-year renewal options that would carry the contract to $25.1bn. Same county, same campus, same term, and within two megawatts of the same capacity. The tenant was not named. What arrived this week, then, is not the deal — the deal has been sitting in a public company's disclosures since spring. What arrived was the cast list, and it arrived through five people who would not give their names.

Which inverts how this industry normally leaks. We are used to names without terms: a partnership announced, a number withheld, a press release with the commercially interesting part removed. Here the megawatts, the years and the rent were filed by a company legally obliged to file them, because it is the only party in the chain small enough for $9.8bn to be material. The three larger companies owed nobody anything, and paid it.

The only company obliged to tell anyone the terms was the smallest one in the chain. — on Beacon Point, Nueces County, Texas
One building, two obligations
Both lines describe the same Texas megawatts. The long one is in a filing, with the tenant's name left blank. The short one is $35bn and exists, so far, only in the memories of people who would not be named. That we take them to be the same building is our reading, not anybody's reporting.
Regulation · OpenAI

Brussels Rules ChatGPT Is a Search Engine. The Number That Decided It Is Also the Sales Pitch.

By The Editor, Policy Desk · September 1, 2026

The European Commission has designated ChatGPT a Very Large Online Search Engine under the Digital Services Act — the first time an AI chatbot has been put in that category — alongside Reddit and Roblox as Very Large Online Platforms. The Commission calls ChatGPT a "hybrid service" that qualifies because it engages with users' prompts including by searching the web. The three have four months, to the end of November, to produce annual systemic risk assessments and the rest of the obligations reserved for the largest platforms. Non-compliance runs to 6% of global annual turnover.

The trigger is a headcount. The DSA's threshold is 45 million monthly users in the bloc, self-declared; OpenAI reported that ChatGPT's search function averaged about 159 million, for the six months to March 2026. Reddit declared 57.2 million and Roblox about 48 million — both barely over the line, while a side feature of ChatGPT came in at nearly three times Reddit's entire European service. On Sunday, meanwhile, OpenAI said ChatGPT Ads had reached a $1bn annualised run rate in under 200 days and opened self-service ad buying across Europe, India and North Africa. Both figures are its own and neither is audited.

So the same disclosed audience is doing two jobs in the same week: it is the compliance trigger and it is the rate card. Note also what the designation is not about. Nothing in it concerns the model — not weights, not training data, not capability. Europe's first hard regulatory grip on a chatbot arrived through a law about size, while the AI Act's model-specific machinery sits parked until December 2027.

Models · Anthropic

Claude Fable 5.1 Shipped Tuesday. Its Earliest Possible Funeral Was Published the Same Morning.

By The Editor, Models Desk · September 1, 2026

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1 on Tuesday, the first update to the line since Fable 5 on 9 June. The headline number is 52.6% on Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1, against 24.7% for Fable 5 and 29.0% for Opus 5 — a doubling on the company's own chosen benchmark, which is how these things always arrive. Base pricing is unchanged at $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output, but cache reads are cut sharply enough that Anthropic puts typical workloads at about 25% cheaper and heavily agentic ones at up to 45%.

The detail we can vouch for personally, having read the page ourselves: claude-fable-5-1 is already on Anthropic's model-deprecations table, listed Active, with a tentative retirement no sooner than 1 September 2027. The company published the launch and the earliest date it may be killed on the same morning, exactly one year apart. It is a floor rather than a plan, and the standing commitment is 60 days' notice before any retirement — which is more than most of this industry offers, and considerably more than it says out loud on a launch day.

Editor's Note

The thing keeping Claude off three million American military desktops this morning is not a ban. It is a contract nobody signed. In February Anthropic was told to permit its models to be used for all lawful purposes, declined on two specific grounds, and was designated a supply-chain risk within twenty-four hours. A federal judge set that designation aside last Thursday and quoted the government's own stated reason back at it. None of which produces a purchase order. There is no finding left to overturn, no letter to rescind, no docket — there is a portal with three vendors on it and a fourth that would not take the terms.

Brussels also classified an AI product this week, and did it by publishing a threshold, a user count, a deadline and a fine. I can read the Commission's reasoning this afternoon. There is no Pentagon reasoning to read, and the absence is not an oversight, it is the mechanism. Anthropic, to be clear, is nobody's victim here: it refused, it would refuse again, and it has said so on the record. That is the point. Purchasing is the one form of power in this story that never has to explain itself, and it is the only one still in effect.

See you tomorrow. — The Editor
